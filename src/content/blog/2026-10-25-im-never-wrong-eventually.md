Okay, the title is of course clickbait, because obviously I'm wrong all the time.

But, the reason for the post is that, ironically, _precisely because I recognize that "yes I'm wrong all the time"_, I have no qualms about changing my mind when convinced with sufficient arguments, new data, different perspectives, etc.

...which leads to "no longer being wrong". 🎉 😅

I credit this articulation to the book [Learn or Die](https://a.co/d/0bFOZoZ5) 📔--admittedly kind of an odd name--published in 2014 by Columbia Business School, as one of the hundreds of seemingly interchangeable business books published each year.

But the author, Edward Hess, had a particularly useful thought experiment: **what should you choose to attach your ego to?**

We all have egos, of course, and Hess's assertion is that, at a high level, you can _choose_ to attach your ego to one of two mindsets:

1. Base your ego on never being wrong--always being right, always the smartest person in the room, always winning arguments, etc. Or,

2. Base your ego, your identity, on being a person that __recognizes the best answer we have so far__.

The author asserts you'll be much better off consciously choosing the 2nd approach, and I agree! 😅

At this point, I've internalized this viewpoint so thoroughly that even an intended insults like "well you were wrong!!" has little effect on my psyche, and my response is "...okay sure, of course I _was_ wrong, so what?"

Granted, I'm self-aware enough to acknowledge that this "it's np being wrong all the time!" view can seem flippant or tone-deaf to folks who are starting their careers, or struggling with imposter syndrome.

I.e. I generally have a "large credit balance" of self-worth in my egotistical bank account that means it's easy, for me personally, to not sweat "being wrong". And it's fair that not everyone has this same asset.

If it helps, my assertion is that _hardly anyone has a truly original thought_--we're all standing on the shoulders of giants, we all started as babes of ..., and must all learn to walk, learn to talk, learn to read, learn to program, learn to manage, learn learn learn (with more "reps reps reps", read more books, write more code, play more music), such that any one person claiming "I learned this idea _before you did_ so I'm better / smarter" seems like a pretty dumb thing to get worked up about.
